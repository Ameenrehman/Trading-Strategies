F2b. IS THE REVERSAL REAL, OR IS IT BID-ASK BOUNCE?
==============================================================================
Every informative feature above has a NEGATIVE IC: yesterday's winners
lose tomorrow. Before believing that, it has to survive the artifact that
manufactures it. A closing print lands at either the bid or the ask, so
close(D) carries a random error. That same error raises ret1 (which
divides BY close(D-1) and multiplies by close(D)) and lowers the next-day
return (which divides by close(D)) - producing reversal out of pure noise.

The control: score the same features against targets that share no price
with them. 'oc' starts at the next open; 'cc(+1)' starts a full day later.
If reversal is an artifact it collapses; if it is real it persists.

==============================================================================
F4. MAGNITUDE - 'how much will it move?'
==============================================================================
Direction and size are not equally hard. Testing them separately:

  today's ATR%   -> tomorrow's |return|         r =  0.305   R2 =  0.093
  today's ATR%   -> tomorrow's high-low range   r =  0.491   R2 =  0.241
  today's TR/ATR -> tomorrow's |return|         r =  0.129   R2 =  0.017
  model score   -> tomorrow SIGNED return       r =  0.018   R2 =  0.000
  model sign agrees with realised sign            50.6 %

Read the two lines above together. Size is forecastable and direction is
not - which is what makes ATR-derived SL/TP legitimate even when the
directional call is weak. It also means any 'it will go up 4.2%' number is
a volatility estimate wearing a direction it has not earned.

the barriers: a tight target with a wide stop wins often and loses big.

F6. THE HIGH-WIN-RATE CANDIDATE - buy the close, sell the open
==============================================================================
F1 found the only leg with a naturally high hit rate: the overnight move
wins 63% of the time on a random liquid name, with no model at all. That
is the closest thing in this data to 'a big win percent', so it deserves
its own test rather than being buried in an average.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Now falsify it. A 71% win rate at t = 20 is not a discovery, it is a
warning: nothing in liquid equities predicts direction that well. The
features carrying it are gap_today (IC +0.098), atr_pct (+0.096) and
vol_ratio (+0.062) - a volatility-and-spread portrait, not a forecast.

If the overnight gain is real it is a permanent repricing and survives the
next session. If it is the bid-ask spread - the closing auction printing
near the bid, the opening trade near the ask - it reverses immediately and
is unreachable, because crossing that spread is exactly what buying costs.

overnight close(D)->open(D+1)              39.8   23.12       19.0     +20.9    20.05
session   open(D+1)->close(D+1)           -27.2   -8.27      -10.2     -17.0    -7.01
full day  close(D)->close(D+1)             12.1    3.41        8.6      +3.6     1.39

5 days    close(D)->close(D+5)             59.3    4.47       42.7     +16.5     2.88

The same picks hand back 83% of the overnight edge during the next session.
What is left over a full day is +3.6 bps at t = 1.39.

The mechanism, shown directly - sort the universe by volatility and watch
the two legs move in opposite directions in lockstep:

    ATR% quintile     overnight bps   session bps   full-day bps
    Q1                         12.2          -5.0            7.0
    Q2                         15.4          -7.8            7.4
    Q3                         17.2          -8.3            8.7
    Q4                         19.7          -9.6            9.9
    Q5                         24.5         -11.9           12.2

The overnight column rises with volatility and the session column falls by
almost the same amount. That is the spread being paid, not an edge being
earned - wider-spread names show a bigger fake gap and a bigger fake fade.
Confirmed on real traded prints: on the 50 symbols with 5-minute data, the
overnight is +5.5 bps measured close-to-open on daily bars, but only
+2.7 bps and 52.2% from the 15:25 print to the 09:20 print - the two
prices you could actually transact at.

==============================================================================
F8. HOLDING PERIOD - is one day the right horizon?
==============================================================================
The 1-day edge is real but small, and a 1-day trade pays a full round trip
to collect it. This scan holds the SAME out-of-sample picks longer, so any
improvement comes from the horizon rather than from a new signal.

t is computed on NON-OVERLAPPING windows (every k-th date), which is the
honest denominator for a k-day hold - overlapping windows reuse the same
market days and inflate t by roughly sqrt(k).

==============================================================================
VERDICT
==============================================================================
Four questions were asked. They get four different answers, and collapsing
them into one number is how a strategy gets built on the wrong one.

1. CAN NEXT-DAY DIRECTION BE PREDICTED?  Weakly, yes - and it is real.
   Out of sample, IC +0.0248 (t 9.40); the top 5 names beat the
   equal-weight universe by +11.7 bps/day (t 5.23), hit 51.6% against
   a 50.5% base rate. It survives the bid-ask-bounce control and is
   positive in 9 of 10 out-of-sample years. It is also about one point of
   hit rate. That is what a genuine daily-bar equity signal looks like.

2. IS IT A TREND SIGNAL?  No - it is the exact opposite.
   Every informative feature has a NEGATIVE IC. Yesterday's strongest names
   underperform tomorrow; distance above the 20-DMA, RSI, consecutive up
   days and closing near the 20-day high all predict WEAKNESS. A screener
   that buys strength is on the wrong side of the only effect present.

3. CAN IT SAY HOW FAR A STOCK WILL MOVE?  Size yes, direction no.
   ATR predicts tomorrow's range with r = 0.49; the model predicts tomorrow's
   SIGNED return with r = 0.018 and agrees with its sign 50.6% of the time.
   So ATR-derived SL/TP levels are well founded, and any '+4.2% expected'
   number would be a volatility estimate with a direction stapled to it.

4. CAN THE WIN RATE BE BIG?  Yes, trivially, and it will mean nothing.
   The barrier grid moves win rate from 46% to 80% without changing a single
   pick - purely by widening the stop against the target. Random selection
   reaches the SAME win rates. Win rate measures barrier geometry; only
   expectancy measures skill.

REJECTED - the 71% overnight strategy. Ranking predicts the overnight move
   with IC 0.16 and a 71.5% hit rate, then hands most of it back the next
   session, leaving +3.6 bps at t = 1.39 over a full day. It is the
   bid-ask spread being measured, not captured. See F6.

NOTE ON TIMING. Buying at the next OPEN - the literal 'list for tomorrow'
workflow - returns +1.6 bps/day absolute (universe -10.2). The drift in
this market is overnight; a list acted on at the open has already missed
it. Deciding and buying into the same close is a different strategy with
a different answer, and both are reported above rather than averaged.

Costs were excluded by instruction, so every figure here is an upper bound.
For scale only: the measured round trip at small size is 27-104 bps, against
a 1-day edge of ~12 bps. The horizon, not the signal, is what has to change.
